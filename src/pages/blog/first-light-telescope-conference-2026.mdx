On October 17, 2026, I had the pleasure to give the opening talk of the 20th First Light Telescope Conference, sadly not in Portland, but remotely from France. It goes through the telescopes I've built since 2019 and the fracture with which ones I actually ended up using.

It was an honor to present among those seasoned opticians and makers.

Why the MPCC and not the four-element GSO corrector that I have in Wallace? Just because of the weight: the MPCC is lighter, shorter, more compact. And by the way, the GSO four-element corrector, which was designed by Roger Ceragioli, is quite underrated. If I go back to Wallace, it has this corrector, we can see just a bit of it peeking out in the light path, and I'm using it exclusively with this 17 mm eyepiece. I mentioned it to various friends who observe and they were a bit doubtful about it, but the correction is quite great, and it logically has a lot less false color than the MPCC.

So if you want to build very small, very wide field telescopes, this corrector can take quite short focal ratios, on the condition that you space it correctly. It's mostly sold with an eyepiece holder that positions the eyepiece at 55 mm of back focus, which is very wrong for it. There are threads on Cloudy Nights with the corrector designer giving information on how to space it correctly, and when you get the right back focus it has a lot of punch for its price. To put it simply: don't discount it, it's not the Paracorr, but it has some power. So, I'm going for a hyperbolic 6" Newtonian.
